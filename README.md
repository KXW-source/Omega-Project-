# Omega-Project-
An open research project exploring whether AI-based systems could improve the administration of complex human societies while remaining controllable, accountable, and corrigible. The author of the article writes in Spanish; therefore, the English translations may contain grammatical errors.





Overview:

Project Omega is an open research program that seeks an ideal system for human governance, proposing an AI system based on precise programming (avoiding the "black box" problem). 

Project Omega aims to address the following two issues:

1. The inefficiency of both humans and AI in the administration of human affairs. 

2. The development of a new, ideal system for the administration of human affairs. 

The points presented here are informal ideas that require further development and research by a range of specialists. 

Readers are invited to analyze, reflect upon, and critically examine each of these ideas—in every possible way—to help arrive at an ideal system of governance.


The Problem:

Large-scale governance involves coordinating competing interests, allocating resources, processing information, evaluating evidence, managing uncertainty, and making decisions whose consequences may affect entire populations.

Human governance systems can experience problems such as:

Conflicts of interest and institutional incentives that diverge from public welfare.

Corruption, information asymmetries, and unequal influence over decisions.

Limited information-processing and coordination capacity.

Short-term incentives that conflict with long-term objectives.

Inconsistent policies, institutional fragmentation, and difficulties in evaluating outcomes.

Failures of accountability, transparency, and institutional correction.


AI-based administrative systems introduce a different set of potential problems, including:

Objective misspecification and optimization of inappropriate proxies.

Biases or errors inherited from training data and system design.

Limited interpretability and difficulties auditing complex decisions.

Vulnerabilities to manipulation, adversarial inputs, and technical failures.

Concentration of computational resources and decision-making power.

Unclear responsibility, legitimacy, and mechanisms for contesting decisions.

Risks associated with excessive autonomy and inadequate human oversight.


These are broad problem categories, not claims that every governance institution or AI system exhibits every failure. Their prevalence, severity, and possible remedies must be assessed using appropriate research and evidence.


Propuesta omega:

¿Could a carefully designed computational governance architecture manage human governance, or at least improve certain decision-making and coordination processes, while limiting the risks of power concentration, system failures, manipulation, and unaccountable optimization?

There is an ongoing debate regarding what constitutes efficient administration and what defines the common well-being of the population. 

The primary proposal is the "Omega system"—a framework of AI agents based on the precise manipulation of concepts (distinct from machine learning-based AI), utilizing a mathematical theory referred to here as "objectual theory." 

This approach avoids the uncertainties—such as self-sabotage or failures (e.g., hallucinations, biases)—common in traditional AI agents, as the underlying computational or decision-making processes can be explicitly analyzed. 

Furthermore, these systems would be programmed with a specific set of immutable goals that serve as the foundation for their processing and behavior. 

These goals are aligned with efficient human administration, ensuring that every action taken by the system serves to benefit the population. The agents' processing includes built-in filters—such as the projection of scenarios or consequences and the weighting of these outcomes against internal goals—as well as an "immune system" (comprising mutual oversight and integrated mechanisms that ensure coherence between the system's goals and its operations). 

This is designed to minimize the risk of self-sabotage, both for the Omega system itself and for humanity. 

The proposal includes specialized "Omega agents"—for instance, the automation of the development of mathematical theories, theoretical models, and technological designs, using computers built upon objectual theory. 

It should be noted, however, that this remains, for now, purely speculative. 

The Omega system is also envisioned as an autonomous species, capable of self-replication and expansion into outer space. 

While this could assist humanity by facilitating the transport of resources, it also gives rise to a challenging debate regarding the allocation of resources: 
specifically, the tension between the requirements for the Omega system’s replication and the resources needed for human administration, human demands, and the needs of the human population. 

To date, neither the validity of objectual theory nor the feasibility of these Omega systems has been demonstrated; the concept remains a foundational idea aimed at addressing the challenges of human administration. 

If it is shown that Omega is invalid, either in whole or in part—whether due to its theoretical or empirical foundations—the project will shift direction toward a different framework, as the core objective is to identify a system of human governance that is as efficient as possible for the benefit of all.

How to Contribute:

Criticism and collaboration are welcome. Particularly useful contributions include:
Relevant scientific papers, books, and documented case studies.
Counterexamples that challenge the project's assumptions.
Existing systems or theories that address similar problems.
Formal models, simulations, and proposed evaluation methods.
Security analyses and failure scenarios.
Political, legal, economic, and ethical objections.
Suggestions for narrowing the scope or rejecting unsupported claims.

To contribute, open a GitHub Issue describing the specific claim, assumption, or mechanism under discussion. Whenever possible, include sources, reasoning, counterexamples, and a proposed alternative.
For substantial changes, explain what problem the change addresses and how it could be evaluated.


Project Principles:

The project aims to follow these principles:

Epistemic transparency: 
Distinguish established findings, interpretations, and hypotheses.

Explicit uncertainty: 
State what remains unknown and avoid unsupported claims.

Traceability: 
Maintain references and a history of substantive revisions.

Critical evaluation: 
Treat criticism as a means of testing the proposal, not merely promoting it.

Revisability:
Allow individual components or the entire approach to be rejected when evidence warrants it.

Accountability: 
Treat legitimacy, oversight, rights, and responsibility as core research questions rather than assuming that technical performance resolves them.


First, an essay will be published regarding the shortcomings of both humans and current AI in the context of governance. 

Subsequently, the full development of the "Omega" system will be released. 

Additional versions of the essay will be uploaded as the framework’s development progresses or as it is reformulated in response to critique.
