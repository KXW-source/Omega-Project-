# Omega-Project-
An open research project exploring whether AI-based systems could improve the administration of complex human societies while remaining controllable, accountable, and corrigible. The author of the article writes in Spanish; therefore, the English translations may contain grammatical errors.





Overview:

Project Omega is an open research program that seeks an ideal system for human governance, proposing an AI system based on precise programming (avoiding the "black box" problem). 

Project Omega aims to address the following two issues:

1. The inefficiency of both humans and AI in the administration of human affairs. 

2. The development of a new, ideal system for the administration of human affairs. 

The points presented here are informal ideas that require further development and research by a range of specialists. 

Readers are invited to analyze, reflect upon, and critically examine each of these ideas—in every possible way—to help arrive at an ideal system of governance.




Omega proposal:



Project Principles:

Epistemic transparency: 
Distinguish established findings, interpretations, and hypotheses.

Explicit uncertainty: 
State what remains unknown and avoid unsupported claims.

Traceability: 
Maintain references and a history of substantive revisions.

Critical evaluation: 
Treat criticism as a means of testing the proposal, not merely promoting it.

Revisability:
Allow individual components or the entire approach to be rejected when evidence warrants it.

How to Contribute:

Criticism and collaboration are welcome. Particularly useful contributions include:
Relevant scientific papers, books, and documented case studies.
Counterexamples that challenge the project's assumptions.
Existing systems or theories that address similar problems.
Formal models, simulations, and proposed evaluation methods.
Security analyses and failure scenarios.
Political, legal, economic, and ethical objections.
Suggestions for narrowing the scope or rejecting unsupported claims.

To contribute, open a GitHub Issue describing the specific claim, assumption, or mechanism under discussion. Whenever possible, include sources, reasoning, counterexamples, and a proposed alternative.
For substantial changes, explain what problem the change addresses and how it could be evaluated.
